Four went in at noon. One came out.

Field note · the Ruined Library, at the door · dusk

The orcs burned this place long ago, and it still hasn't finished burning. You can smell it in the stone.

Four of us went in at noon. I came out alone. The others are somewhere between the shelves, and I am out here writing, because writing is the only thing my hands will do without shaking.

What I know: one goblin. Small. Quick. It doesn't fight like a soldier. It fights like a child who found a knife and made up a game. It bit through the Archer's crossbow string.

What I don't know: why it stays. Goblins don't keep anything. This one keeps the red books.

If you are reading this, you are about to go in. Stay low. Leave the red ones be until it looks away.

I will finish this note when I find them. — N. B.

Sit by the campfire and Natalie tells it to you herself. The bold words swell, the whispers go small, and the fire flares on the hard parts.

the quest

Where did Natalie's party go?

Natalie hands you the Archer's crossbow and ten bolts, and the library door unlocks. Around the town, the camp and the stacks, her friends left things behind. Find at least three, open your casebook with J, and write down what you think happened.

Your theory decides where you look. Natalie follows your lead, whether the evidence backs it or it's a leap.

the town

Every building is a place to make something

You wake in the inn, or on a bedroll by the fire. Nine buildings sit round a cobbled plaza with a fountain, by a river with a stone bridge, a windmill and the library spire. Townsfolk walk, spar, haggle and chat. Walk up to a building and its workshop opens beside you.

sources disagree

Every teller is a little bit wrong

Artheria's history is written by its people, and they don't agree. Gods, demons, goblins, even the name of the land each have two or three versions, and the game keeps all of them. Pick a question and decide who you believe.

    “Where my sources disagree, I let them. Choosing one teller over another is a job for whoever comes after me.” — Natalie

    the chronicle

    How the world got this way

    The order below is the one the old books mostly agree on. Mostly.

    1. Before memory

      The gods in the golden bath

      White-winged spirits bathe in golden flame, ringed by swirls of gold and marble. They tend the few thinking creatures, tire of watching them repeat their mistakes, and start coming down in pairs to play with mortals like toys.

    2. The First

      Ein walks home

      One god says it is wrong. The others strip Ein of his rings and throw him down. He crosses storms and wars, shows his six-winged form on the tallest peak and flies home for years. Then he chooses to come back, breaks the Gods' Bath, hides his rings and dies a mortal among friends. One by one the other gods fall after him.

    3. The outcasts

      Elves bear round-eared children

      Short-lived and unable to use magic, the first humans are cast out. With the halflings they build a society of stone, find what they take for this world's own magic, and found the Library of the Wise.

    4. The burning

      The orcs come back

      Cast out of human society in their turn, the orcs return in great numbers and great anger. They burn the Library of the Wise. It has been the Ruined Library ever since, and it has never quite stopped smouldering.

    5. The Era of Ice

      The promise that shattered the world

      Losing the war, a wizard called the Scholar summons the Patron, a shape-shifting thing that speaks in feelings. Its invisible fire warms human homes through the cold. The sky goes grey, demons overrun the orcs, and the price curses the Scholar's bloodline for generations. Two portals remain: Noctavel and Emberkin.

    6. Now · dusk

      A stagecoach pulls into town

      A small town has grown up beside the ruin. Natalie Bourbon, collecting everything for her book, sits by the library door with the Archer's crossbow; her party is lost inside. A shard of the Noctavel Portal still sits on the scorched altar in the back hall. The fire never reached it. You step down from the coach.

    the red books

    Twelve shelves. Not every book survived.

    Red books are lore that friends wrote and the library kept. Grey ones are what the fire, the damp and the bookworms left. Pull one out.

    the colours of magic

    Magic shows you where it came from

    Every spell burns the colour of its source. Raw, unshaped magic glows blue; everything else carries the mark of whoever it was drawn from. Choose a flame.

    somewhere between the shelves

    The goblin

    One of the Biomancer of Nier's soldier strain, bred to win by numbers: grown in five years, gone in twenty-five. It sees well but not low, and it hoards the red books. Down in the dungeon, every goblin is rolled from trait tables and gets a name.

    by the dojo door

    Kavik

    Purple skin, horns, a wrap top and hakama. He stands beside the dojo watching the pupils spar. Challenge him and the camera swings side-on for a best-of-three bout. He goes easy until you win once, then fights fair.

    the curio pedlar

    Magister Greer

    “Wizard, collector of curios, and only occasionally a rooster.” Show him what you carry and he'll tell you what it's worth in words, like the shard of the Noctavel Portal or the Archer's crossbow. Words are cheap. His prices are not.

    spirit of Straphan Forest

    The Fauna God

    The woods around the town belong to it. Its spirit walks Straphan Forest as a white deer, and beasts and giants are its creatures. Travellers who keep still at sunset sometimes see it at the treeline. Nobody has seen it come closer.

    ruler of the low-magic frontier

    The Stagecoach

    A tan, bald giant of a man. They say he never walks; the ground moves out of his way. His coat is stitched from seven stolen flags, and every larder he passes is empty, which may explain the ration sack by the camp.

    the look

    A storybook you can walk around in: painted ground, red roofs, ink lines, and a sky laid down in soft bands.

    Drawn faces

    Characters have realistic proportions and low-poly heads with the face drawn on. Six expressions, plus blinking and talking, swap like painted face textures. They take torch, campfire and lantern light, and dim with the hour.

    Painted ground

    The meadow carries soft brush strokes at two crossing scales. Bushes are a few fat lumps, flowers come in big cream, gold and pink clumps, and every tree is a crossed card, like a stage set.

    Ink and lamplight

    A thin ink line runs round every character, building and prop. The sky is painted in flat bands. At sunset all 26 lit windows start to glow and the lanterns come up along the paths.

    Write in the book so the next traveller hears you.

    Anyone can add to the world: a story, a character, a weapon, a song, a recipe or a painting. Unfinished fragments are shelved on purpose.

    Write lore at the scriptorium or from Extras → Write lore. Make a character at the tailor's and a weapon at the anvil; both come with constraint dice when you're stuck. Your work stays on your device until you choose Submit, and it's credited exactly as you sign it.

    Up to three profiles per device, each with its own hero, save, bag and writing. Export one and take it to another machine.
