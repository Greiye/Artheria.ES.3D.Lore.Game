# The Elves, Expanded

*Part of [[Peoples]] · from* Tales of Artheria

The elves come from a primitive homeworld. Their thousand-year lives let them witness the events that shaped their history, among them the creation of humans, a race whose uncanny likeness (save the ears and height) left the elves both intrigued and unsettled. Elven literature is of unparalleled quality: scholars of every race treat elven texts as invaluable, meticulously researched and accurate.

## Elven Society

Elves don't build in the middle of nowhere. They find a **mana source** and live close to it, often in a cave. The less unnatural construction the better, though elven building isn't ruled out. They then build whatever is needed to maintain the mana ecosystem: protecting the animals and trees, and stopping any threat that would poison or kill the source.

Elves prefer harmony with their environment but understand necessity. They have the infrastructure of any society: waste management, agriculture and so on. Population is kept low and constant so as not to strain the source, with failsafes such as **child licenses**, and exporting elves to other settlements when there are too many or too few.

With other sentient races threatening their way of life, the elves founded the **Capital**, their most advanced society, built with the technology of a bygone era. The Capital's mana source is artificial, and looks less natural than any other elven settlement's.

## Elven Duty: "Protect the Manasource"

Every elf learns their community's mana source and is taught to protect it from outsiders. A threat is first reported to the council, and a team is dispatched to watch it. If the outsiders plan harm, they are sabotaged: the watch team stays hidden the entire time, steals tools in the night and sets traps that look like bad luck. The moment an elf dies, the threat is to be destroyed outright, with the whole commune.

## The Elven Life

1. **Birth.** Elves give birth like any other mammal, but need a touch of natural mana.
2. **Community care.** The community raises the young while the parents serve the commune.
3. **The first spell.** Elven children mature slowly. Humans walk and talk within 3 years; elven children take something like 30. Tied to magic, elven children often manipulate mana before they can talk.
4. **Giving to the community.** From 30 to 130, children who want to help can do clean-up or supervised gathering. There are stories of parents woken from meditation because their child was in the mood to pick up trash at midnight.
5. **The scribe years.** From 60, children are taught the world and magic, and may go on road trips to other elven settlements. This lasts until about 170.
6. **Fend for yourself.** Elves learn combat when they turn rowdy, usually between 60 and 120. The final test is to hunt an animal. If it is killed, every part must be used. If it is captured, it must be tamed; if every taming technique fails, it is released. At 170, they are formally taught advanced combat, the threats of the world, other races' fighting styles and offensive magic. Training is strict, and when it is time to fight, the community fights as one.
7. **The Pilgrimage.** At 200, an elf should leave and explore the world. Pilgrimage groups of 4 to 6 close friends travel, work and sightsee in other elven lands, binding the settlements together. Few young elves forego it.
8. **Finding a mate.** Elves are encouraged to mate with another elf. Those who mate with another race are likely excommunicated, judged case by case.
9. **The golden centuries (200–900).** By 180, an elf looks as they will for the rest of their life: perfectly symmetric, clear-skinned, thick-haired. Only their mana changes, and other elves can sense it, which they call the **Elven Sense**.
10. **Settling.** Around 500, elves settle in their home community and start families. In the Settling ritual, other elves build their home for them. They then volunteer with the Commune Council wherever they fit best.
11. **The memoir.** Between 500 and 800, elves are encouraged to write down their lives and whatever useful knowledge they found, and submit it to the Capital. If the Capital needs more, it sends a recorder.
12. **The Passing.** Elves disappear slowly. Most have written their wills long before.

Elven mating is an intricate ritual that uses a great deal of magic.

## The Capital and its Secrets

The Capital is believed to be the landing site of the Travelers. Beneath it is a maze of tubes and objects that houses its mana source, the **Fairy Starship**. At its heart, the **Grand Academy** holds the rust-covered metallic structure, guarded by golems and opened only by the royal circlet.

The Capital is accessible only to full-blooded elves. Something of spirit or blood bars every other race from entering, half-elves included, and no one is sure what becomes of non-elves who pass the barrier. The **Capital Spies** keep its location secret through disinformation. Any elf who reveals even the border of the Capital is destroyed without a trace, and their fate is covered with misinformation. In class, elves are taught to speak of the Capital only in layers of security (magical tools that replace the speakers' words, or a guarded dialect), only with those they trust, and never before other races, orcs above all.

**Government.** A theocratic monarchy. The King, Queen, princes, princesses and a few knights form a tight royal family. The King holds immense power through the golems but is mostly ceremonial; local councils, spiritual leaders, mana guardians and elders run the smaller settlements. Leadership is often hereditary but can be earned through magical prowess, wisdom or service to elven culture. Within settlements, society is fairly egalitarian, and elders and mana specialists are most respected.

**Economy.** Small, self-sufficient populations focus on agriculture and upkeep. Elven literature, textiles and magical artifacts are prized abroad, though the elves rarely trade at scale. Select outsiders may be allowed into their libraries.

**Military.** Archers, spellcasters, spearmen, soldiers and spellswords; nearly every elf is trained in combat magic and hand-to-hand fighting. The **Capital Army** is not an invasion force but a militia with an army's strength, there to protect the library and the mana source. The King's golems guard the Capital itself.

**Arts.** Elven texts are the highest form of literature, written for both future elves and other races' scholars. Elves cherish slow, methodical music and ritual dance for the seasons and for the Passing. They also study other cultures' dances, on the principle that to know your enemy is to be able to crush them.

## Elven Magic and Subcultures

Elves absorb their surroundings, and their magic takes on the texture of the biome that raised them. Desert elves' magic is grainy and sandy; forest elves', leaves and twigs. Dark elves' magic can't be seen, only felt, like hairs rising on your neck. Marine elves' magic smells of salt and humid warmth. In a sense, an elf is a quarter spirit and three quarters animal. Each subculture speaks a dialect close to its own language.

- **Wood Elves.** The main line during the ancient purges. Masters of detection, stealth, slings, arrows and spears, from a time when giant beasts roamed beneath the trees.
- **Dark Elves.** Cave dwellers with poor eyesight and a keen sense of touch and vibration. Their magic is exploratory, feeling out the dark with tendrils. Expert spelunkers and hunters of cave insects; they dress in stretchy climbing gear and know how to rappel.
- **Capital Elves.** Used to cobblestones, trade and settled life, they are weaker and slower than natural elves but know more spells and more weapons. **City Elves** are similar, but scorned for living among other races.
- **Tundra Elves.** Solitary, in frigid pockets, living in caves with igloos outside, eating fish caught through the ice.
- **Desert Elves.** Draw strength from sand and heat, eating plants, insects, small mammals and carrion.
- **Marine Elves.** Live with the tides in coastal or underwater settlements, eating spear-hunted fish and sea plants.

## The Passing

An elf's passing begins when they turn intangible: hands and objects slip through them. This lasts up to a week. The elf may sit, meditate, walk or speak calmly while friends and family gather to say goodbye; though they may not react, they still hear their name. Afterwards a **bonfire** is lit and their belongings are burned in it. The metal and ash are collected, melted down and passed to the next of kin, so the elf returns to the world through the things they loved.
